# Adversarial review — foveated_optics_study

## 1. [CONFIRMED] The radial map uses ONLY the temporal meridian; the advertised "~2x margin over the retina" collapses to ~1.07x (essentially zero) in the nasal-inferior periphery, and ~30% of the panel's lenses sit outside any field direction the FOV actually uses

`scripts/foveation_target.py:1-11` states "F follows the Watson (temporal meridian) midget-RGC pitch **everywhere**", and `panel_radius_mm`/`target_pitch_rad` (L38-48) depend only on eccentricity `theta`, never azimuth — the map is perfectly radially symmetric. But the *acceptance* tolerance, `blur_tolerance_rad` (L56-59), correctly uses the anisotropic `retina_model.square_equivalent_pitch_deg`, which varies strongly by meridian (measured, `retina_model.one_mosaic_spacing_deg` at ecc=35°: temporal 11.53′, nasal 21.23′, superior 18.12′, inferior 23.02′ — up to 2x).

Reproduced numerically (script in this review): scanning the whole 70x45° field, `target_pitch_rad(ecc) / blur_tolerance_rad(tx,tz)` (the actual oversampling margin the panel delivers) ranges from **2.39x** near the temporal meridian down to **1.066x** at the far nasal-inferior corner (tx=-35°, tz=-22.5°) — barely above the Nyquist minimum of 1, versus the single number "SAMPLING_OVER_RETINA ≈ 1.98" quoted in the module docstring and in `screen_spec.py`'s header comment. Any additional real-world softening (assembly tolerance, MLA hex-vs-square lattice mismatch, remapper residual distortion) can push that corner under 1, i.e. genuine aliasing, with no headroom.

Separately, because the map is purely radial onto a *square* panel while the FOV is rectangular (70x45°, not 70x70°), inverse-mapping a uniform grid over the physical panel back to field angle shows only **69.7%** of panel area (and by extension lens count) ever corresponds to an in-FOV direction; the rest (≈91,000 of the ≈302,700 lenses tiling the panel at 36 µm pitch) is dead. The header comment "About 300,000 lenses, against the ~340,000 retina-matched samples" (`screen_spec.py` docstring) compares the *raw* lens count to the anisotropic ideal sample count as if it were an 88% match; the real usable comparison is closer to 211,000/338,000 (≈62%).

**Fix**: make the map's local scale depend on azimuth (interpolate `one_mosaic_spacing_deg` over all four meridians, as `retina_pitch_rad` already does for the tolerance, instead of only `"temporal"`), and/or state the true worst-case margin instead of the on-axis one.

## 2. [CONFIRMED] The diffraction floor (Airy first-null radius) is not commensurable with the RMS-angular-width blur metric it clips

`foveation_target.py:30,56-59`: `AIRY_RAD = 1.22 * lambda / D` (the classic first-null/Rayleigh radius, 0.577′ for D=4mm) is used as a hard floor on `blur_tolerance_rad`, which is otherwise an RMS pitch, and `lf_evaluate.metrics` / `fold_search.cell_blur2` both score blur as an **RMS** angular width. These are different statistics of a PSF. Verified numerically here: integrating the normalized Airy intensity `I(x) ~ (2 J1(x)/x)^2` against `x^2` (the RMS second moment) over increasing radial cutoffs gives 6.9, 32.5, 127.6, 637, 3213 for cutoffs 10, 50, 200, 1000, 5000 — growing essentially linearly with the cutoff, i.e. the Airy pattern's RMS width **diverges** (its 1/x^3 tail has no finite second moment). The 1.22λ/D figure is instead an *encircled-energy* radius (confirmed: 84.0% of energy inside it here, matching the textbook 83.8%). Comparing a genuinely RMS-defined blur metric to a divergent-second-moment quantity's finite encircled-energy radius is a category error; a self-consistent RMS-equivalent diffraction floor (e.g. a Gaussian core fit) would be markedly smaller. Practical impact is limited to the very fovea (Airy 0.577′ only barely exceeds retina 0.53′ there per `test_blur_tolerance_is_the_retina_clipped_at_the_diffraction_limit`), so this doesn't move most of the field, but the "no optics can do better" framing overstates the floor's rigor.

**Fix**: either drop to an MTF-cutoff-based or fitted-Gaussian RMS floor, or switch the acceptance metric itself to encircled-energy to match the Airy quantity being clipped against.

## 3. [SUSPECTED, root-cause] Both independent remapper searches (fold_search, gpu_search) plausibly fail not because of search bugs but because the foveation target's F0/F35 ≈ 21.8x magnification range and 49 µm lenslet focal length are outside what a few low-order freeform/aspheric surfaces can deliver

`fold_search.py:60,285-291`: `RATIO_BAND=(0.85,1.2)` on the Jacobian singular values (weight 30, higher than the blur weight of 1) forces the local magnification to track `F(theta)` at every sampled field point, and `run()`'s LM acceptance (`ok = alive_all==1.0 & ...`, L446) only keeps steps where every FD-probe ray for the ratio check stays alive (`okd`, L269-273 folded into `alive_all`), so the search cannot simply "kill" the fovea probe to dodge the ratio residual — this closes the obvious loophole. Yet the reported best 1-corrector design misses its own ratio target 4-6x at the fovea (0.26x vs [0.85,1.2]) while doing comparatively better in the periphery (1.1-1.8x) — a genuine inability to reach the target, not a scored-but-ignored violation. `gpu_search.py:124-136` similarly forces every pupil ray for a field to converge on one point `ty` (an aberration-free-imaging condition, `err2` weight 1 plus a 9x chief-ray term) yet the best reported design is 2.8x tolerance blur **at the fovea itself** (ecc≈0°), where a simple coaxial system usually has the least aberration to correct — consistent with the fovea's tolerance being unusually tight (≈20 µm RMS spot at F0=117.8mm, D=4mm) rather than with a coding bug. I did not rerun the GPU search (told not to; GPU in use) so this is inference from the reported numbers and the residual weighting, not a reproduced experiment. A cheap falsifying test: rerun either search with the magnification range artificially capped (e.g. F0 clamped to 5x F35 instead of 21.8x) and see if per-pixel blur converges near tolerance; if it does, the target itself is the bottleneck, not the optimizer.

## 4. [CONFIRMED by code, SUSPECTED in effect] fold_search's own blur/ratio proxy is self-referential: it uses its own finite-difference Jacobian (0.1° step) both to define the local-magnification target and to convert the same design's pixel-plane spread into angle space

`fold_search.py:232-237` (`jacobian`) and `239-256` (`cell_offsets`/`cell_blur2`): `ang = jac^-1 @ dev` — a systematic Jacobian error (e.g. because the true mapping is strongly nonlinear within one 0.1° step, which is exactly the failure mode this design has) enters both the "ratio" residual and the "blur" residual computed from the same linearization, so an inaccurate local model cannot be caught by comparing it against itself. This is consistent with the task's observed fact that the best design's blur proxy (0.7-0.9x tolerance at 1-10°) is markedly better than what an independent Cycles evaluation of the exported design would likely show once real ray divergence (not the FD-linearized version) is used — I did not run the Blender-based cross-check (prohibited here), so the actual size of the discrepancy is unverified; the mechanism is real (verified by reading `residuals()` L258-291, which only ever calls `jacobian()`/`cell_offsets()` with the design's own FD Jacobian, no independent verification pass).

**Fix**: validate the search's own blur proxy against a smaller FD step (or the exact ray-traced spot, `per_field_blur` already exists at L365-374 without gradients) and report the discrepancy alongside the merit at each checkpoint, not just at the end.

## 5. [SUSPECTED, minor] Per-pixel blur attribution in `lf_evaluate.metrics` credits a shared pixel's whole variance to a single, view-order-determined "owner" lens, and silently drops any lens/pixel that never accumulates ≥2 rays

`lf_evaluate.py:191-239`: `first[pix]` is fixed to the entering lens of whichever ray (in ascending-pupil-radius view order) reaches that pixel first; all of that pixel's accumulated RMS spread (`width2`, mixing rays from every lens that also lands there) is then credited entirely to `first[pix]` (L233-239), and any lens whose pixels are all "claimed" by another lens under crosstalk gets zero measured pixels of its own → `blur=inf` for that lens. Separately, pixels reached by <2 of the 61 pupil samples (`hit = pix_n >= 2`, L229) are excluded from `width2` entirely — with only 61 hex-lattice samples over the 4mm pupil, this is precisely most likely in the badly-blurred periphery (rays spread over more distinct pixels → fewer rays per pixel). Both failure modes push toward `blur=inf`, which (via the "nearest rank" percentile, L280-282 comment) inflates `blur_pitch.p90` and would *fail* acceptance rather than hide a bad design — so the direction of the bug is safe for the accept/reject gate, but it means the reported per-lens/per-field blur breakdown (used to steer which region of a design to fix) can misattribute blame under strong crosstalk. Not independently reproduced against Blender output; flagged from code reading only.

## 6. Tests: mostly solid ray-geometry checks, but a few are circular or under-sampled

- `tests/test_foveation_target.py::test_the_display_samples_the_retina_at_one_constant_factor` (L54-62) is a tautology: `target_pitch_rad(theta)/one_mosaic_spacing_deg(theta) = LENS_NEIGHBOUR_MM/(F0*p0)` is constant *by construction* of `local_focal_mm` (L33-35), independent of the Watson formula's correctness. `test_blur_tolerance_is_the_retina_clipped_at_the_diffraction_limit` and `test_focal_length_follows_watson_over_the_whole_field` likewise re-derive the exact formula under test rather than checking it against an independent source (e.g. hand-computed Watson values at a couple of eccentricities, or the physical F0/F35 numbers this review reproduced). None of these would catch a wrong Watson coefficient or a wrong Airy prefactor.
- `tests/test_export_fold.py::test_the_baffle_hides_the_hardware_from_the_pupil` (L98-118) only checks lines of sight from 3 fixed pupil points ((0,0),(0,1),(1,0), i.e. centre, top, right) against a random sample of hardware vertices — a gap visible from the bottom or left edge, or a diagonal corner of the pupil, would not be caught by this test. The comment claims to check "every line of sight" but the implementation samples a tiny, non-adversarial subset of viewpoints.
- The RMS-width bias correction used in `lf_evaluate.metrics` (`width2 = 2*(1-R/n)*n/(n-1)`, L229-232) was Monte-Carlo verified here (not in the repo) to be accurate to ~1% at small angular spread and to ~6% at 0.15 rad (8.6°) spread, independent of n down to n=2 — this part of the evaluator checks out and is *not* a finding.
