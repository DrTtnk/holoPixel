# Foveated Holographic Rendering: Literature Survey

Prepared for the HoloPixel project (150 PPI true holographic pixel display, 512×512 hogel grid, 256 px hemisphere per hogel, Sb2Se3 phase-change modulator, 3-bit phase quantization, head-mounted target).

## Verdict

Foveated holographic rendering exists as a named research topic, but it is a small, young, single-lineage field, not a mature discipline. Two research groups — Chakravarthula/Fuchs (UNC Chapel Hill, later Princeton) and Akşit/Ritschel/Weyrich/Urey (UCL/Koç University) — produced essentially all of the directly relevant work, concentrated in 2021–2022, with the field otherwise dormant since. Both groups worked on a single, flat, gaze-tracked 2D image plane driven by one conventional phase-only SLM. Neither group, nor anyone else we found, has published a foveation scheme for a hogel/light-field holographic architecture like ours: nobody varies hogel density with eccentricity, nobody varies per-hogel hemisphere resolution with eccentricity, and nobody varies phase bit-depth with eccentricity. Critically, nobody has measured a compute or wall-clock saving from foveating a hologram computation. Every "foveated CGH" paper we found reports image-quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, HDR-VDP, user-study preference), never FLOPs, iteration count, or render time attributable to foveation itself. The single most useful paper for HoloPixel is Walton et al., "Metameric Varifocal Holograms" (arXiv:2110.01981), because it is the only one that (a) runs on real holographic display hardware, (b) reports actual optimization wall-clock numbers, and (c) demonstrates a transplantable acceleration mechanism (warm-starting the iterative solver from the previous frame's phase pattern) that is architecture-agnostic and could plausibly be applied to a hogel-grid solver. Everything else in the field is either a quality trick that costs the same compute (Chakravarthula et al., arXiv:2108.06192) or comes from adjacent, non-holographic rendering (foveated NeRF, foveated rasterization) whose eccentricity-dependent sampling ideas are conceptually transferable but have never been ported to coherent-light rendering. The honest one-line summary: **the specific problem HoloPixel needs solved — cutting the compute of a hogel-grid CGH pipeline by shrinking hogel density and/or hemisphere resolution with eccentricity — has not been attempted by anyone**, in holography or in any adjacent field. That is a gap, not a dead end.

## Mechanisms (question 2): proven vs. speculative

### Spatially varying hogel density
**Status: no published work.** The only "hogel" paper we found in this space, Hogel-Free Holography (DOI:10.1145/3516428), argues for *removing* hogels altogether rather than foveating them, on the grounds that hogel/stereogram methods inherently trade spatial against angular resolution. Nobody has published a scheme that keeps a hogel grid but thins it with eccentricity.

### Spatially varying angular (hemisphere) resolution
**Status: proven only in a non-holographic analogue.** FoV-NeRF (arXiv:2103.16365) varies ray/angular sampling density with eccentricity in an egocentric neural radiance field and gets up to 99% time reduction at perceptually matched quality. This is architecturally the closest thing to "shrink the per-hogel hemisphere from 256 px to something smaller in the periphery," but it was never done for a coherent wavefront computation, and NeRF ray sampling and Fresnel/angular-spectrum hologram propagation have very different compute-scaling behavior (the former is embarrassingly parallel per-ray; the latter typically uses FFTs whose cost depends on the full transform size, not just how many "useful" pixels you keep).

### Foveated CGH optimization with eccentricity-weighted loss
**Status: proven for image quality, not for compute.** This is the one mechanism with real papers: Chakravarthula et al. (arXiv:2108.06192, Eq. 8–9) multiply the per-pixel MSE loss by a mask derived from Watson's 2014 midget-retinal-ganglion-cell density formula, and Walton et al. (arXiv:2110.01981) replace pixelwise MSE with a Freeman-and-Simoncelli-style metameric loss over steerable-pyramid statistics whose pooling-region size grows with eccentricity. Both are demonstrated to work (95%+ objective noise reduction and majority-preference 2AFC results for Chakravarthula; real-hardware reconstructions for Walton). Neither reduces the number of pixels computed, the SLM resolution, or the number of solver iterations because of foveation — the loss is reweighted, but the forward model (`propagate()` in Walton et al.'s Listing 2) still runs over the full image every iteration. The only speed number in either paper (90 s → 4 s, Walton et al., Sec. 3.1) comes from warm-starting the solver with the previous frame's phase pattern when gaze and focus barely move, which is a temporal-coherence trick orthogonal to the eccentricity weighting.

### Sparse/pruned point clouds outside the fovea
**Status: no CGH-specific work found; proven in adjacent point-based rendering.** MetaSapiens (arXiv:2407.00435, point-based neural rendering) prunes Gaussian/point primitives more aggressively in the periphery and reports an order-of-magnitude rendering speedup with a user study confirming no perceived quality loss. This is a rasterization/point-splatting pipeline, not a hologram; nobody has applied per-point pruning to a pointwise CGH method (the kind Hogel-Free Holography or Maimone et al.'s classic pointwise CGH use), even though the mechanism looks directly portable — a peripheral 3D point simply contributes less phase-optimization effort.

### Varying phase bit-depth by eccentricity
**Status: no published work of any kind.** We found nothing — not even a proposal — that reduces SLM/phase-modulator bit-depth with eccentricity. All CGH bit-depth work we found (e.g. Time-multiplexed Neural Holography, arXiv:2205.02367; Endo et al.'s INT8-quantized hologram network, arXiv:2409.06711) quantizes uniformly across the whole field for hardware or network-efficiency reasons unrelated to gaze.

### Multiplexed fovea+periphery optical paths
**Status: proven for conventional (incoherent) near-eye optics, not for coherent/holographic combination.** The classic reference here (cited inside arXiv:2108.06192 as ref. [1], Akşit et al., "Manufacturing application-driven foveated near-eye displays," IEEE TVCG 25(5):1928–1939, 2019) builds a foveated display by physically combining a high-resolution foveal projector path with a wide-FOV peripheral path using a beam combiner — but the peripheral and foveal images are conventional pixel-based images, not two coherently combined holographic wavefronts. Nobody has published a scheme that interferes or multiplexes two separate coherent wavefronts (one high-detail foveal hologram, one coarse peripheral hologram) at the optical-combination stage.

## Perceptual models and the speckle problem (question 4)

Two distinct families of eccentricity-falloff model appear in this literature, and neither was built for, or validated against, coherent speckle:

1. **Anatomical receptor-density models** (Watson 2014's mRGC density formula, used directly by Chakravarthula et al., arXiv:2108.06192, Eq. 6). This model describes how densely the retina *samples* the image, which is a reasonable proxy for resolvable spatial frequency, but it is a static, non-temporal, single-observer-averaged model. Chakravarthula et al. say so themselves in their Discussion (Sec. 6): "Our current model considers the retinal sampling density that relates to noise detection [rather] than discrimination... Extending our approach to also reflect the spatio-temporal effects of human perception is an exciting future research direction." In plain terms: they used a model of *how finely you can see*, not a model of *how annoying random speckle looks*, and they flag that gap as unsolved.
2. **Contrast-sensitivity-function (CSF) models built on natural, incoherent imagery**, used by the rasterized-foveated-rendering literature (surveyed in arXiv:2211.07969 and arXiv:2205.04529) and by Walton et al.'s metameric-statistics loss (arXiv:2110.01981), which is itself derived from Freeman & Simoncelli's ventral-stream texture-metamer model, calibrated on natural photographs. Krajancich, Kellnhofer, and Wetzstein (arXiv:2302.01368) further show that even for ordinary incoherent CSF, the periphery's real tolerance depends on how much visual attention the task is pulling toward the fovea — the classic eccentricity-only CSF over-estimates how much you can get away with. None of this CSF work has been re-derived or re-validated for fully-developed speckle, which is a multiplicative, negative-exponential-statistics noise process, structurally different from the additive, 1/f-like statistics of natural image texture that these models were fit to.

**We found no psychophysical study anywhere that measures speckle-noise detectability or discomfort as a function of retinal eccentricity.** This is the single largest unaddressed question standing between "we have a CSF model" and "we know how aggressively we can foveate a coherent, speckled hogel display." It is explicitly called out as unsolved by the one paper that comes closest to the problem.

## Eye-tracking latency budget (question 5)

The literature does not converge on a single hard number, and we could not independently verify the commonly-cited "50–70 ms" figure from Albert et al.'s 2017 ACM TAP paper (it is not on arXiv, and we did not access it directly, so we do not repeat its number as fact). What we *can* report from sources we actually read:

- Kwak et al., Saccade-Contingent Rendering (arXiv:2401.16536), hardware-measure a window of **several hundred milliseconds of suppressed visual acuity immediately after a saccade**, and show that on a 90-PPD headset, observers cannot tell reduced-resolution rendering from full resolution during that window. This gives a genuine, measured slack window — but it only helps immediately after large eye movements, not during steady fixation, which is when most of a CGH solver's iteration budget would need to run.
- Feng et al., BlissCam (arXiv:2404.15733), show that eye tracking itself can consume roughly half of a mobile VR device's power budget and that tracking latency directly competes with the rendering latency budget; their in-sensor 20× downsampling buys back 8.2× energy and 1.4× latency.
- Xin, Wang, and Zhang, A3FR (arXiv:2507.04147), show that if gaze tracking and rendering are not parallelized, tracking overhead can outweigh the rendering savings that foveation was supposed to buy — a systems trap, not a perceptual one.

**No paper anywhere asks whether a CGH phase-retrieval solver can fit inside any of these budgets.** The two existing CGH speed regimes are: (a) iterative gradient-descent solvers, which the field's own numbers put at 90 s cold and ~4 s warm-started for a single 2D image on a 2080 Ti (Walton et al., arXiv:2110.01981) — both far outside any plausible frame budget — or (b) fast feed-forward networks such as Shi et al.'s tensor holography (Nature 2021, cited but not independently re-verified by us here) which run in milliseconds but at a fixed architecture and fixed output resolution with no eccentricity-adaptive compute graph. Nobody has built or measured a CGH network whose compute graph itself shrinks with eccentricity the way a foveated rasterizer's G-buffer does.

## Adjacent work worth stealing (question 6)

- **Stanford (Wetzstein group):** Neural Holography (Peng et al.), Time-multiplexed Neural Holography (arXiv:2205.02367), Gaussian Wave Splatting for CGH (arXiv:2505.06582), Stochastic Light Field Holography (arXiv:2307.06277, with Meta/Northwestern), Towards Attention-aware Foveated Rendering (arXiv:2302.01368). Strong on camera-calibrated forward models and fast differentiable solvers; weak/absent on foveation of the hologram itself.
- **Meta Reality Labs (Lanman/Maimone/Matsuda group):** Pupil-Aware Holography (arXiv:2203.14939), Neural Étendue Expander (arXiv:2109.08123), HoloChrome speckle reduction (arXiv:2410.24144), Saccade-Contingent Rendering (arXiv:2401.16536, rasterization). This group has the most real hardware experience with large-FOV, retinal-resolution coherent HMDs, and the most credible latency/perception data — but their foveation work (Saccade-Contingent Rendering) is rasterization, and their holography work (Pupil-Aware, Étendue Expander) is not foveated.
- **UCL/Koç University (Akşit/Ritschel/Weyrich/Urey group):** Metameric Varifocal Holograms (arXiv:2110.01981), Hogel-Free Holography (DOI:10.1145/3516428), HoloBeam (arXiv:2212.05057). This is the group whose combined output is most directly relevant to HoloPixel: one paper gives the only real foveated-CGH hardware demo, the other gives the strongest argument against a bare hogel architecture.
- **UNC Chapel Hill → Princeton (Chakravarthula/Fuchs/Heide group):** Wirtinger Holography, Gaze-Contingent Retinal Speckle Suppression (arXiv:2108.06192), Hogel-Free Holography, Ellipsography (arXiv:2604.16237, 2026 — a direct intellectual successor addressing speckle via a different, vectorial-interference mechanism; worth a follow-up read once effort budget allows).
- **MIT Media Lab / CSAIL (Matusik group):** Near-eye light field holographic rendering with spherical waves (Shi et al., TOG 2017, cited inside arXiv:2108.06192 ref [50]) and End-to-end learning of 3D phase-only holograms (Shi, Li, Matusik, Light: Sci. Appl. 2022, DOI:10.1038/s41377-022-00894-6) — the tensor-holography lineage, fast feed-forward 3D CGH, but not gaze-adaptive.
- **Foveated NeRF / point-based rendering, as a mechanism donor rather than a holography paper:** FoV-NeRF (arXiv:2103.16365) and MetaSapiens (arXiv:2407.00435) are the two best quantified demonstrations that eccentricity-scaled *sampling density* (angular rays, point pruning) can cut compute by 90%+ with no perceived loss, in pipelines that are structurally closer to a hogel/light-field renderer than ordinary rasterization is. These are the strongest candidate templates to adapt, even though neither touches coherent light.

## What this means for a 512×512 hogel, 256-px-hemisphere pipeline

- **No published technique reduces the compute of a hogel-grid CGH renderer.** Every number in this literature is either a quality metric (PSNR/SSIM/HDR-VDP/2AFC preference) or, in one case, a warm-start speedup unrelated to eccentricity. Any foveation gain for HoloPixel's 512×512 × 256×256 ≈ 17.2 Gpixel workload will have to be measured from scratch; there is no existing benchmark or reported percentage to calibrate expectations against.
- **The one transplantable, quantified trick is temporal warm-starting.** Walton et al. cut their per-frame solve from 90 s to ~4 s (roughly 22×) by initializing an iterative SGD solver with the previous frame's phase pattern instead of random noise, when gaze and focus change little between frames (arXiv:2110.01981, Sec. 3.1). If HoloPixel's hogel solver is iterative, warm-starting each hogel's hemisphere from its own previous-frame solution — more aggressively for peripheral hogels whose content changes least in retinal-relevant terms — is a directly applicable, low-risk mechanism, and it is orthogonal to (can be combined with) any eccentricity-based resolution reduction.
- **FoV-NeRF's up to 99% time reduction (arXiv:2103.16365) is the best available quantitative anchor for "how much might per-hogel hemisphere resolution reduction save,"** but it is an extrapolation, not a measurement: FoV-NeRF's cost scales with the number of camera rays traced, while a hogel's 256×256 hemisphere is typically computed via an FFT-based angular-spectrum or Fresnel propagation whose cost does not fall linearly with a masked-off output region unless the underlying transform size is also reduced (e.g., by computing peripheral hogels at a genuinely smaller FFT size, not by discarding pixels post-hoc). This distinction — ray-based cost models transfer to hogels only if the hologram compute itself uses a comparably sub-samplable representation — is the first thing to verify before assuming any percentage from the rasterization/NeRF literature will materialize in an FFT-based hogel pipeline.
- **Hogel-Free Holography's critique (DOI:10.1145/3516428) is a strategic warning, not just a citation.** It argues that a fixed hogel grid inherently wastes angular resolution wherever it is not needed and spatial resolution wherever the eye does not look — exactly the inefficiency foveation is meant to fix — but its own proposed fix is to abandon the hogel representation for a pointwise/wavefront method scaling with scene point count. Before investing in foveating the existing 512×512-hogel architecture, it is worth explicitly costing out whether a pointwise or hybrid approach, foveated by point density instead of hogel density, would be simpler and reach the same target: this is a real fork in the design space, not a side note.
- **Speckle mitigation via eccentricity weighting (arXiv:2108.06192, Eq. 8–9) is applicable to HoloPixel as a quality lever, at zero compute cost,** by weighting each hogel's hemisphere reconstruction loss by that hogel's angular distance from the tracked gaze direction — but it will not shrink the 17.2 Gpixel/12.6 s number, only make the existing pixels look better where it matters.
- **The 3-bit / 8-level Sb2Se3 phase quantization and the 0.5 µm sub-pixel pitch have no precedent anywhere in this literature.** Every hologram-quality number reported (PSNR, SSIM, HDR-VDP, speckle-suppression percentages) was measured on continuous-phase or ≥8-bit liquid-crystal SLMs with pixel pitches of several micrometers. None of it transfers quantitatively to a 3-bit phase-change-material modulator; if eccentricity-dependent phase bit-depth is attempted (mechanism candidate 5 in the task brief), it will need its own perceptual validation from a clean slate, since phase-quantization noise (a structured, periodic-lobe artifact) has different statistics from both natural-image blur and Gaussian-PSF speckle, and no published falloff model addresses it at any eccentricity.

## Gaps: where nobody has gone (this is where HoloPixel's contribution would sit)

1. **No eccentricity-varying hogel density**, for any hogel/light-field holographic display. The one relevant "hogel" paper argues for eliminating hogels, not foveating them.
2. **No eccentricity-varying per-hogel angular/hemisphere resolution** for a coherent (holographic) pipeline. The only proof this class of idea works at all (FoV-NeRF) is in incoherent volumetric rendering.
3. **No eccentricity-varying phase bit-depth**, at any bit depth, for any modulator technology. Completely unaddressed.
4. **No coherently-multiplexed fovea+periphery optical path.** The one multiplexed-optics foveated display we found combines two conventional (incoherent) image paths, not two interfering coherent wavefronts.
5. **No measured compute/FLOP/wall-clock saving from foveating a CGH pipeline of any kind**, against any baseline. Every "foveated CGH" result to date is a quality-only result.
6. **No psychophysical model of speckle detectability or discomfort vs. eccentricity.** The nearest paper explicitly flags this as future work it did not do.
7. **No latency co-design between an eye tracker and a CGH solver.** All existing tracker/solver latency co-design work (A3FR, BlissCam, FovealNet) targets rasterization or point-splatting renderers, whose latency profile (single-pass, sub-millisecond-to-low-millisecond per frame) is qualitatively different from an iterative CGH solver's (tens of seconds to milliseconds, depending on whether it is optimization-based or feed-forward, with no established middle ground that also adapts its compute graph to gaze).
8. **No published foveation work of any kind on a Sb2Se3 (or any PCM) phase-change modulator**, or on any sub-1-µm-pitch holographic pixel array. The entire perceptual-holography literature assumes conventional liquid-crystal SLM hardware.

Any one of items 1, 2, or 3 above — demonstrated on real hardware with a measured wall-clock or FLOP number, which item 5 shows nobody has ever reported — would be a genuine first for this field.
