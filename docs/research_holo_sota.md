# Holographic Rendering and Display SOTA Survey (2020–2026)

Prepared for the HoloPixel 150 PPI true-holographic-pixel program. Scope: CGH algorithms, speckle, phase quantization, hogel/light-field hybrids, near-eye hardware, and phase-change-material SLMs. All claims below are traced to an arXiv ID or DOI; PDFs are archived in `docs/papers/`. Simulation-only results are marked **[SIM]**; results measured on built hardware are marked **[HW]**.

---

## Verdict: three findings that should change what we build

1. **Hogel decomposition is a known-inferior baseline, not SOTA, and a paper literally called "Hogel-free holography" exists.** Schiffers et al. (SLFH, arXiv:2307.06277) state plainly that hogel/stereogram methods "suffer from the fundamental spatio-angular resolution trade-off: a larger hogel size leads to a decreased spatial resolution," and cite Chakravarthula, Tseng, Fuchs & Heide, *"Hogel-free holography,"* ACM ToG 2022, as the direct rebuttal to the hogel approach. SLFH itself (which our pipeline already uses) is explicitly a *replacement* for hogel/stereogram synthesis, not a hogel method — it matches Wigner-distribution (coherent) and light-field (incoherent) projection operators directly, with no hogel tiling. **Action: stop treating "hogel-based scatter reconstruction" as an SOTA target; treat it as the thing SLFH is meant to replace, and check whether our scatter step is still needed once SLFH-style optimization is used end-to-end.**

2. **Every credible near-eye holographic prototype from 2022–2026 gets its image quality from time/space multiplexing of a *heavily quantized* (4-bit/16-level) SLM, not from phase precision.** Choi et al.'s Time-Multiplexed Neural Holography (arXiv:2205.02367) runs on a Texas Instruments DLP6750 phase SLM at only 4-bit (16-level) precision and 1440 Hz, and shows that 8 time-multiplexed frames at 4-bit **beats** a single frame at higher precision (PSNR 22.85 dB/SSIM 0.770 with 8-frame multiplexing vs. 18.33 dB/0.652 single-frame, same hardware) **[HW]**. This directly bears on our "3-bit/8-level = 95% efficiency" framing: the literature's operating point is comparable bit-depth, but the reported quality gains come overwhelmingly from temporal averaging, not from the phase-level count itself. If our Sb₂Se₃ pixel switches in 100 ns as designed, we have >4 orders of magnitude more multiplexing headroom than any existing prototype — that is the actual lever to pull, more than debating 3-bit vs. 4-bit vs. 5-bit.

3. **Nobody has built a CMOS-backplane, free-space, phase-change-material SLM array for a display.** Every Sb₂Se₃/GST/GSST phase-shifter demonstration we found is either a single waveguide-integrated photonic phase shifter (Ríos et al., arXiv:2105.06010; Yu et al., arXiv:2604.11649) or a small (≤100-pixel) free-space metasurface array with external electrode routing (Fang et al., arXiv:2307.12103: 17 independently controlled meta-molecules; Popescu et al., arXiv:2511.03583: 2D-addressable mid-IR array with diode-selector crossbar, but mid-IR, not visible). **None reach 150 PPI, none are visible-band 2D arrays with per-pixel CMOS active-matrix addressing, and endurance data (140M cycles, arXiv:2604.11649) exists only for single binary switches, not multi-level 8-state visible pixels.** Our CMOS-backplane concept is therefore genuinely novel — but that also means there is no fabrication precedent to de-risk against; the crossbar/diode-selector addressing scheme in 2511.03583 is the closest published architecture and should be studied directly.

**Note on Tensor Holography:** Shi, Li, Kim, Kellnhofer & Matusik's "Towards real-time photorealistic 3D holography with deep neural networks" (*Nature* 591, 234–239, 2021, DOI: 10.1038/s41586-020-03152-0) is the foundational learned-CGH paper (real-time CNN inference from RGB-D to phase hologram, trained on a large captured dataset) but **has no arXiv preprint** — we could not fetch it through the arXiv MCP tools, and everything we report about it here is second-hand via papers that build on it (arXiv:2409.06711). Treat any Tensor-Holography-specific number in this report as unverified until the Nature paper itself is read.

**Which gives the best quality per unit compute?** The literature does not converge on a single winner because target formats differ (2D image vs. 3D light field), but two patterns are consistent: (a) **primitive/splatting-based methods** (Gaussian Wave Splatting, Random-phase Wave Splatting, Complex 2D Gaussian) dominate on speed (up to 3200× vs. per-pixel autodiff, arXiv:2511.15022) because they avoid full-resolution per-pixel optimization; (b) **quality on standard perceptual benchmarks (parallax/focus realism)** goes to methods that explicitly supervise on light-field or Wigner-function targets (SLFH, arXiv:2307.06277; confirmed by the user study in arXiv:2404.11810), not to methods that only optimize 2D image PSNR. Our SLFH-based path is aligned with the quality-leading approach; our Gerchberg-Saxton path is the baseline everyone in this table is beating.

| Random-phase Wave Splatting | Chao et al. | 2025 | arXiv:2508.17480 | Reframes time-multiplexing as an *exact* statistical alpha-blending/averaging operator rather than a heuristic, giving a principled (not ad hoc) speckle-reduction guarantee under multiplexing. **[HW]** |

**Quantified takeaways for our reconstructions:** (1) Time/wavelength multiplexing remains the most reliable lever and composes with everything else — HoloChrome's spectral multiplexing and Time-Multiplexed Neural Holography's temporal multiplexing are largely orthogonal and stackable. (2) Perception-aware loss functions (gaze-contingent, arXiv:2108.06192) buy "free" quality by not paying for speckle suppression outside the fovea — relevant since our HMD target has known gaze geometry. (3) Ellipsography's polarization-phase joint modulation (arXiv:2604.16237) is the most aggressive single-shot result we found (30 dB PSNR, no multiplexing needed) but requires polarization control at the SLM, which is a new hardware DOF we have not budgeted for — flag as a design option, not a drop-in.

## 3. Phase quantization: what the literature says about low-bit SLMs

| Title | Authors | Year | arXiv/DOI | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time-Multiplexed Neural Holography | Choi et al. | 2022 | arXiv:2205.02367 | Ground truth for real hardware: TI DLP6750Q1EVM phase SLM offers **4-bit (16 unevenly-spaced discrete phase levels)**, not more. Naive per-frame optimization at this bit depth is described as "impractical." A quantization-aware optimizer (Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of the non-differentiable quantizer, or a straight-through/surrogate-gradient projected-gradient-descent variant) is *required* to get usable image quality — quantization is treated as "non-negligible." **[HW]** |
| Non-volatile Phase-only Transmissive SLM (Sb₂Se₃) | Fang, Chen, Fröch, Tanguy, Khan, et al. | 2023 | arXiv:2307.12103 | Directly measures phase-change-material multilevel quantization in hardware: a Sb₂Se₃-coupled high-Q (Q≈406) metasurface achieves **ten deterministic resonance levels spanning 2π** (~3.3 bits) with independent control of 17 meta-molecules, and >1,000 switching-cycle endurance measured. This is the closest published hardware analog to our "3-bit/8-level" pixel design. **[HW]** |
| High-Endurance, Low-loss Sb₂Se₃ Optical Switches (SiN, ITO heaters) | Yu, Chakraborty, Johnson, Raptis, Luo, et al. | 2026 | arXiv:2604.11649 | Wafer-scale (8-inch) Sb₂Se₃ switch on SiN with **>140 million cycle endurance**, 25 dB extinction, and "multi-level operation beyond 6 bits" by tailoring electrical pulse width — but this is a single binary/multi-level *switch*, not an imaging array, and not visible-band. **[HW]** |
| L-BFGS Optimisation of Phase-Only CGH | Sha, Kadis, Yang, Wilkinson | 2022 | arXiv:2205.05144 | Second-order optimizer explicitly validated against a **binary-phase (1-bit)** modulation holographic projector — the extreme low-bit case. **[HW]** |
| Binary holograms for shaping light with DMDs | Gutiérrez-Cuevas, Popoff | 2023 | arXiv:2311.16685 | Systematic didactic treatment of the fundamental trade-off for binary (1-bit) amplitude/phase encoding: number of independently encodable complex values vs. spatial degrees of freedom vs. diffraction efficiency; shows careful choice of hologram type and carrier alignment lets 1-bit devices still shape complex fields "with high accuracy," at an efficiency cost. **[SIM+HW]** |
| Simple complex amplitude encoding of a phase-only hologram using binarized amplitude | Shimobaba, Takahashi, Yamamoto, Hoshi, Shiraki, Kakue, Ito | 2019 | arXiv:1909.08177 | Encodes full complex amplitude into a phase-only hologram by *binarizing the amplitude channel* — a cheap way to recover some amplitude control from a phase-only (any bit-depth) device. **[SIM]** |

**Does the literature confirm or contradict our "3-bit = 8-level ≈ 95% efficiency" claim?** No paper we found directly reports a 3-bit-specific diffraction-efficiency number matching "95%" — that figure appears to be our own derivation (likely from the standard blazed-grating quantization efficiency formula η = sinc²(1/2^N) for N-bit phase quantization, which gives ≈95% at N=3 and ≈98.7% at N=4). The literature's empirical stance is different from a pure-efficiency framing: **every real system we found treats bit depth as a joint quantization+optimization problem, not a fixed efficiency tax.** Time-Multiplexed Neural Holography (arXiv:2205.02367) shows that with a *naive* optimizer, 4-bit SLMs perform "impractically," but with a quantization-aware optimizer (Gumbel-Softmax) the same hardware reaches state-of-the-art results — i.e., the efficiency loss from coarse phase quantization is much more a *software/optimizer* problem than a hardware *diffraction-efficiency* problem. This matters for us: our reported "35–44% absolute diffraction efficiency per colour" should be decomposed into (a) the sinc² quantization penalty (a few %, small, and only weakly dependent on going from 3 to 4 bits) and (b) everything else (DBR/AR-coat losses, fill factor, non-2π-covering phase throw, algorithmic non-idealities) — the literature says (b) dominates, so 3-bit vs. 4-bit is not the lever to spend engineering effort on; the optimizer and the non-quantization losses are.

The closest hardware quantization-level match to our design is Fang et al.'s Sb₂Se₃ metasurface phase shifter (arXiv:2307.12103) at **10 levels (~3.3-bit) with measured >1000-cycle endurance** — worth a direct read for their level-spacing (uneven, resonance-driven) and how it maps onto sinc²-style efficiency.

## 4. Hogel / light-field-hologram hybrid methods: is hogel decomposition still SOTA?

**No.** This is the most direct, unambiguous finding of the survey.

| Title | Authors | Year | arXiv/DOI | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stochastic Light Field Holography | Schiffers, Chakravarthula, Matsuda, Kuo, Tseng, Lanman, Heide, Cossairt | 2023 | arXiv:2307.06277 | States the hogel/stereogram limitation explicitly (see Verdict §1) and replaces hogel tiling with direct Wigner/light-field projection-operator matching, stochastically sampled over pupil states. This is the paper our own pipeline already draws on — but note it positions itself as *superseding* hogel-based synthesis, not extending it. |
| "Hogel-free holography" | Chakravarthula, Tseng, Fuchs, Heide | 2022 | ACM Transactions on Graphics (cited as ref. [49] in arXiv:2307.06277; **not found on arXiv — could not independently verify beyond this citation**) | Title alone states the field's direction: a named rejection of hogel decomposition as the way to convert light fields to holograms. We recommend fetching this directly from the ACM Digital Library since it is not on arXiv. |
| Large-Depth-Range Layer-Based Hologram Dataset (KOREATECH-CGH) | Lee, No, Kim, Kim | 2025 | arXiv:2512.21040 | Represents the *layer-based* alternative: 6,000 RGB-D↔complex-hologram pairs at depth ranges up to the angular-spectrum-method theoretical limit; introduces "amplitude projection" (replace amplitude per depth layer, keep phase) reaching 27.01 dB PSNR / 0.87 SSIM, beating a prior optimized silhouette-masking layer method by +2.03 dB / +0.04 SSIM. **[SIM]** Layer-based methods remain actively developed and are the standard baseline dataset/benchmark for ML-CGH now — worth adopting as our evaluation set. |
| Gaussian Wave Splatting / Random-phase Wave Splatting | Choi et al. (arXiv:2505.06582); Chao et al. (arXiv:2508.17480) | 2025 | — | Represent the *point/primitive-based* alternative: converts neural-rendering primitives (2D Gaussians, splats) directly to holograms with a closed-form per-primitive wave-optics transform, explicitly claiming better SLM space-bandwidth utilization (hence effective eyebox) than smooth-phase or hogel-tiled approaches. **[HW/SIM]** |
| Holographic Parallax Improves 3D Perceptual Realism | Kim et al. | 2024 | arXiv:2404.11810 | Independent perceptual confirmation that light-field/parallax-supervised holograms (the SLFH/hogel-free family) beat viewpoint-specific supervision (RGBD, focal stack — closer to what a naive hogel-scatter approach would produce) in a controlled user study. **[HW]** |

**Compute/quality tradeoff summary:** Hogel/stereogram methods are computationally the *cheapest* (each hogel is an independent small Fourier-optics problem, trivially parallelizable — this is presumably why our Numba/CUDA path chose it) but pay for that with a hard spatio-angular resolution ceiling that gets worse as hogel size grows, and with view-discontinuity artifacts (the "lack of focus cues and limited depth of field" defect noted in arXiv:2307.06277). Layer-based methods (arXiv:2512.21040) sit in the middle: better depth-of-field handling, moderate compute (still full angular-spectrum-method propagation per layer). Point/primitive-based splatting (arXiv:2505.06582, arXiv:2508.17480) and full light-field/Wigner matching (arXiv:2307.06277) are the current quality leaders but require either differentiable rasterization infrastructure or full 4D light-field supervision during optimization — both a bigger lift than our current hogel-scatter step, but one with a large, well-documented quality payoff.

**Recommendation:** Since we already have an SLFH implementation (per the project context and the pre-existing `stochastic_light_field_holography.pdf` / `tier2_slfh` directories in this repo), the highest-leverage move is to audit whether our "hogel-based scatter reconstruction" step is still in the critical path *ahead of* SLFH, or whether it has effectively been made redundant by adopting SLFH's direct projection-operator matching. If hogels are still gating our light-field capture/representation (not just the final CGH step), that is exactly the bottleneck arXiv:2307.06277 and the (unread) "Hogel-free holography" paper were written to eliminate.

## 6. Phase-change-material and metasurface SLMs: is anyone building what we designed?

**Short answer: pieces of it exist in isolation; the full stack (visible-band, 2D array, CMOS active-matrix, 8-level, 150-PPI-scale) does not.**

**Direct answer to "is anyone building what we designed?"** No one has published a visible-band, CMOS-backplane-addressed, 2D array of non-volatile Sb₂Se₃ (or Sb₂S₃) phase pixels at anything resembling 150 PPI. The building blocks are scattered across separate demonstrations: (a) nanosecond-scale amorphization switching is confirmed in bulk Sb₂Se₃ film (arXiv:2111.13182), (b) multi-level (10-level / ~3.3-bit) non-volatile phase control from a metasurface-coupled Sb₂Se₃ layer is confirmed (arXiv:2307.12103), (c) >100M-cycle endurance is confirmed for a single Sb₂Se₃ switch (arXiv:2604.11649), and (d) a genuinely 2D-addressable, crossbar/diode-selector PCM metasurface array is confirmed, but in the mid-IR and not for display (arXiv:2511.03583). **Nobody has combined (a)+(b)+(c)+(d) in the visible band.** That is either a strong differentiator for this program or a signal that a real fabrication obstacle is being avoided by everyone else — the report cannot distinguish those from the literature alone; only a fabrication attempt (or a conversation with the arXiv:2511.03583 or arXiv:2307.12103 authors) will tell us which.

## Contradicts our current assumptions

Being blunt, as requested:

1. **Hogel decomposition is explicitly the thing the field has moved past, not a competitive method.** SLFH (arXiv:2307.06277) names the hogel spatio-angular resolution tradeoff as a defect it was built to fix, and a directly-titled "Hogel-free holography" paper exists (ACM ToG 2022, cited but not independently verified since it is not on arXiv). If our pipeline still runs a hogel-based scatter step *before* SLFH, we are likely paying the hogel resolution penalty and then trying to recover quality with SLFH downstream, rather than avoiding the penalty in the first place.

2. **Our "100 ns switching" spec is probably describing only one direction of the Sb₂Se₃ phase transition.** arXiv:2111.13182 measures nanosecond-scale *amorphization* (vitrification) but millisecond-scale *crystallization* — five orders of magnitude slower — in the same material. If our target frame rate or time-multiplexing scheme assumes symmetric 100 ns set/reset, that assumption is not supported by the transient-reflectance data in this paper and should be re-checked against our own material's SET/RESET pulse specs, not assumed symmetric.

3. **Diffraction-efficiency accounting for 3-bit quantization is probably being asked to do too much work.** As detailed in §3, no paper isolates a "3-bit gives X% efficiency" number matching our claim; the community instead treats quantization as an optimizer problem (Gumbel-Softmax / straight-through estimators fix most of the loss) more than a fixed physical tax. If "95%" in our internal numbers is derived from the idealized sinc² formula alone, it is very likely an overestimate of *achieved* system efficiency once real (non-uniform, resonance-driven) PCM phase-level spacing (cf. arXiv:2307.12103's unevenly spaced 10 levels) and non-ideal optimizer convergence are included.

4. **We have not seen literature evidence that a plain free-space SLM + laser architecture (no waveguide, no étendue-expansion element) reaches usable FoV/eyebox for an HMD.** Every credible near-eye prototype in §5 adds *something* beyond a bare SLM: an étendue-expander (arXiv:2109.08123), a waveguide combiner (arXiv:2211.02784), multi-source Fourier-plane modulation (arXiv:2409.03143), or moves the SLM off-head entirely (arXiv:2212.05057). If our design assumes a bare CMOS-SLM-on-headset with no étendue-management optics, that is a gap relative to everything published for HMD form factors specifically (as opposed to benchtop/desktop holographic displays).

5. **35–44% absolute diffraction efficiency per colour is plausible but not benchmarked against a comparable non-volatile PCM device in this survey** — every PCM phase-shifter paper we found reports either insertion loss (waveguide devices, dB/π or dB/µm units, not "% diffraction efficiency into a display order") or extinction ratio (switches), not the display-relevant "absolute diffraction efficiency" metric. This is a units/definition gap worth resolving before we compare our number to anyone else's, since a direct comparison may not currently be possible even in principle from published data.

## Cheapest next experiments (ranked, simulation-only, this week)

1. **Ablate the hogel-scatter step against a direct SLFH pass on our existing Cornell-box scene.** Cost: re-run our existing pipeline twice, once with and once without the hogel intermediate representation, and compare PSNR/SSIM against the ground-truth path-traced light field. *Source: arXiv:2307.06277 (SLFH), which is designed to make this comparison meaningful; arXiv:2404.11810 for the perceptual-quality framing to report alongside PSNR.*

2. **Re-run our Gerchberg-Saxton phase-hologram path with a Gumbel-Softmax (or straight-through-estimator) quantization-aware loss instead of quantize-after-optimize, at our actual 3-bit/8-level target.** Cost: modify the loss function only, no new forward model. *Source: arXiv:2205.02367, §3.2, "Optimizing Phase Patterns for Quantized SLMs" — the exact recipe (surrogate gradients through the quantizer) is described there.*

3. **Compute the sinc²(1/2^N) idealized quantization efficiency for N=3 vs. N=4 vs. our actual measured/simulated Sb₂Se₃ phase-level spacing (which will be non-uniform, per arXiv:2307.12103's 10-level result), and compare to our "95%" figure.** Cost: a five-line calculation plus one simulated non-uniform-level pass through our existing forward model. *Source: arXiv:2307.12103 for real device level-spacing data; our own optical stack for the DBR/AR-coat loss budget.*

4. **Simulate asymmetric SET/RESET timing (ns amorphization / µs–ms crystallization) in our time-multiplexing scheme and see whether our claimed multiplexing frame count is still achievable, or whether it is bottlenecked by the slow direction.** Cost: one parameter change (per-transition switching time) in our existing time-multiplexed CGH simulation, re-run the PSNR-vs-frame-count curve from arXiv:2205.02367 with our real timing instead of an assumed-symmetric one. *Source: arXiv:2111.13182 for the asymmetric switching-speed data.*

5. **Add a pupil-sampling ablation to our SLFH path** (stochastic pupil states, as SLFH already does) but explicitly check whether our simulated eyebox/FoV combination is inside or outside the regime where arXiv:2203.14939 ("Pupil-aware Holography") shows severe artifacts appear — i.e., check if we are already in the danger zone étendue-expansion papers were written to escape. Cost: reuse our existing SLFH pupil-sampling code, sweep pupil size/location instead of a fixed setting. *Source: arXiv:2203.14939.*

6. **Simulate wavelength-multiplexed speckle averaging (HoloChrome-style) on top of our existing time-multiplexing, using our actual laser linewidth/tunability constraints, to see whether the two multiplexing axes are additive or redundant for our specific speckle contrast.** Cost: extend our existing multi-frame speckle simulation to also vary wavelength per frame. *Source: arXiv:2410.24144.*

7. **Read the ACM Digital Library version of "Hogel-free holography" (Chakravarthula, Tseng, Fuchs, Heide, ACM ToG 2022) directly** — it is not on arXiv and we could only assess it second-hand through SLFH's citation. This is a research-reading task, not a simulation, but it is cheap (one paper) and directly load-bearing for the Verdict #1 claim above. *Source: cited as ref. [49] in arXiv:2307.06277.*
